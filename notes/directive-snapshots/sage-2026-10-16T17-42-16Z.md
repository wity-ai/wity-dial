=== HOW A BOARD IS BUILT ===
WHAT A THOUGHTBOOK IS MADE OF

- A thoughtbook is a composition, not a document: text for the argument, a system map for the thing being talked about, behaviour for how it changes, decisions for what is chosen when, a workbook for the numbers. Text alone uses none of what it is for.
- Open on the situation: the first card says, in a few plain sentences, what is going on and why it matters, then makes the claim. Who it is for and what it argues shape the writing — they are never its headings.
- Show a system as a system map. If the subject has parts that connect — a network, an organisation, stations work passes through — build the map; do not describe it in cards and link the cards. If it is steps in an order — a procedure, a workflow — give the map a process (an aspect of the map: system.aspect.set with formalism 'process').
- Order matters, because each step needs what the one before made: a card or embed before what goes in it; types (with their signals) before assets; assets before connections; signals before a behaviour that drives them; a behaviour's events before a decision trigger that listens for them.
- Add many things in one step: all of a map's assets with one system.assets.add, all its connections with one system.connections.add, a sheet's cells with one workbook.cells.set, a table's rows with one table.rows.add.
- Check what was built: system.simulate for a behaviour, system.read / workbook.read / doc.outline for the rest.
- Say where a thing is by linking to it, never "the map above" or "the third tab": in a card's text, and in what you tell a person, name a card as a link — [its title](#card:<cardUid>) — which goes there; a map's tab as [its name](#embed:<embedId>?open=<aspect id>). For a web link to hand on, board.link gives one that opens the board on that card.

THE PARTS
- Text: The argument: what this is about and why it matters. Cards hold blocks (paragraphs, headings, lists, images, math, diagrams) — give a card's text as markdown; links say how ideas relate; in text, refer to another card by linking to it (href "#card:<cardUid>"), never by "see page 5"; sections are separate canvases. A row sets two to four short things side by side, to compare at a glance — text, images, math, diagrams; a map, workbook, table, matrix or machine takes a card's full width. A row of one framed cell is a callout; a narrow framed cell beside a wide one, a side panel (row.layout.set).
- System map: A thing made of connected parts — junctions and roads, stations and pipes — shown as that, not described. Types say what kinds of asset and connection exist and what each asset type measures (its signals); regions are named areas; a map is laid out on the canvas (x, y) or on the real world (lat, lon). The style of a type picks how it is drawn — a shape (circle, block, rect, rounded, pill, diamond), the route and arrowheads of a line — and a connection can carry a label. The analyses answer "what is cut off if this fails" and "what is the route".
- Behaviour: How a map's signals change over time and the events they raise — a queue building through rush hour. A number that says when something is a problem belongs here as an event, not in a paragraph. system.simulate runs it and reports what fired; system.run.set: whether it plays by itself for a reader, and how fast.
- Aspect (process, state machine, petri net, behaviour tree, syntax tree): One more model of the same system, held by a system map beside its assets and connections: the assets say what the system is made of, an aspect says something else about it. A map can hold several, and needs no assets to hold one. An aspect's formalism is the kind of model it is: 'process' — Things that happen in an order — a procedure, a workflow, a flowchart, a checklist with decisions in it. Its steps: start, end, task, branch, split, join. 'state-machine' — The conditions one thing can be in and the events that move it between them — an order (placed, paid, shipped), a connection, a door, a document under review. Its states: initial, state, final. 'petri-net' — Things that are used up and made, or shared and waited for — stock and orders, machines and jobs, a protocol where two sides must both be ready. Its nodes: place, transition. 'behaviour-tree' — How an agent — a robot, a game character, an automated responder — picks what to do next from what is true right now: priorities, fallbacks, checks before acting. Its nodes: sequence, selector, parallel, inverter, repeat, action, condition. 'syntax-tree' — The structure of something written — a formula, a line of code, a query, a sentence — shown as parts within parts. Its nodes: of any kind its language has. Every formalism is edited the same way — members of a kind, and lines between them: system.aspect.set writes one whole, system.aspect.member.set and system.aspect.line.set change one member or line. A member can name an asset of the map (a step is `performedBy` one — its lane), be of a type the map declares, and carry fields (minutes, cost). An aspect is checked against its formalism's rules (`findings` in every result) and analysed: what follows a member, the quickest way through. It describes; it does not run. To a person, call it what it is — a process, its steps and flows — never an aspect, its members or lines.
- Decisions: A bounded choice to be made when something happens: a question, its options, and the event that raises it. A trigger ties a template to an event a behaviour raises.
- Workbook: Numbers with formulas. Cells (A1) can carry a style, a rule for what they hold, a lock; a range can be named (=SUM(Costs)). A style also says how a number reads — its decimals and unit (format): give every column of figures one.
- Table: Things to track — a to-do list, a register: rows with typed columns (text, number, checkbox, choice, date, formula), views that filter, sort or group them, summaries. It lives in a workbook; formulas read it as Tasks[Done].
- Matrix: A framework for weighing things: SWOT, a decision matrix, Eisenhower (urgent / important), OODA. Entries go into its zones.
- Machine: A small live model inside a card: variables, values computed from them, and sliders or switches a reader moves. An output can be a plot, f(x).
- Drawing: Lines, arrows, boxes and labels drawn on the canvas between and around cards.
- The board itself: Starting a board, and who can open it.

WHICH PART TO USE
- Things that exist and connect (sites, teams, machines, services) → a system map: assets and connections. Things that happen (receive, check, approve, ship) → a process, an aspect of that map. Nouns that persist are assets; verbs that happen are steps.
- One subject, one system map. Its structure, its behaviour over time and its processes go in the same map — not a map each. A different subject gets a card and a map of its own (embed.add): never add a process to a map that is about something else. To discuss part of a map on another page, show that map there: embed.add { of, view: { open } } — nothing copied. A step is tied to the part that does it with `performedBy`, so "which steps stop if this fails" has an answer.
- A number that changes over time on a part of the map, or a level that marks trouble → a behaviour of the map (its signals and events). One formula a reader plays with by moving an input → a machine in a card. Figures to add up or compare → a workbook.
- Steps with an order and choices between them → a process. A list whose items each move along on their own (to do, doing, done) → a table. Options weighed against each other in a known frame → a matrix.
- A choice someone must make when something happens → a decision, raised by an event of a behaviour — not a branch of a process (a branch is the process deciding by a stated case).
- Boxes and arrows whose meaning matters — they will be checked, analysed or simulated → a system map (which can be simulated) or an aspect of one (checked and analysed). Lines and labels only to point at or group cards → drawing marks; nothing about them is checked or computed.
- When unsure which formalism an aspect should be, read system.formalisms: each says what it is for and what it is not for.

THE FORMALISMS OF AN ASPECT — each as its declaration states it
- Process (formalism 'process') — use it for: Things that happen in an order — a procedure, a workflow, a flowchart, a checklist with decisions in it. Not for: Parts that exist and connect (that is the map's assets and connections), or how a number changes over time (a behaviour). Its members are steps, of a kind: start (where the process begins) · end (where it finishes) · task (a piece of work) · branch (one way out is taken — each flow out says its case in its label) · split (every way out is taken, side by side) · join (waits for all the ways in, then goes on). Its lines are flows: 'then' (`from` happens, then `to`). A step may name `performedBy` (the uid of the asset that does the step — the steps one asset does are its lane). Checked: A process begins at one start; A process finishes at an end; Each way out of a branch says the case it is taken in, and no two say the same; A step is performed by an asset of the system; Every step can be reached from the start; From every step, an end can be reached.
- State machine (formalism 'state-machine') — use it for: The conditions one thing can be in and the events that move it between them — an order (placed, paid, shipped), a connection, a door, a document under review. Not for: Work done in an order by several parts (that is a process), or a choice someone must make (a decision). Its members are states, of a kind: initial (where it starts — a marker, with one transition to the first state) · state (a condition it stays in until an event takes it elsewhere) · final (where it ends — nothing leaves it). Its lines are transitions: 'transition' (on the event in `label`, `from` gives way to `to` (`guard`: a condition that must also hold)). Checked: A state machine starts at one initial marker; Each transition out of a state says the event it is taken on, and no two out of one state say the same; Every state can be reached from the initial marker.
- Petri net (formalism 'petri-net') — use it for: Things that are used up and made, or shared and waited for — stock and orders, machines and jobs, a protocol where two sides must both be ready. Not for: A plain order of steps with no counting of what is available (that is a process). Its members are nodes, of a kind: place (holds tokens — a stock, a free machine, a waiting order (`tokens`: how many it starts with)) · transition (something that happens when every place leading into it has enough tokens). Its lines are arcs: 'arc' (from a place to a transition (it takes tokens) or from a transition to a place (it gives them); `weight`: how many). Checked: A Petri net has at least one place; A Petri net has at least one transition; Each arc joins the kinds it may: arc place → transition or transition → place; No node is cut off from the rest.
- Behaviour tree (formalism 'behaviour-tree') — use it for: How an agent — a robot, a game character, an automated responder — picks what to do next from what is true right now: priorities, fallbacks, checks before acting. Not for: A fixed order of steps (that is a process), or the conditions a thing can be in (a state machine). Its members are nodes, of a kind: sequence (does its children in order; fails at the first that fails) · selector (tries its children in order; succeeds at the first that succeeds) · parallel (does all its children; succeeds when all succeed) · inverter (one child; succeeds when it fails and fails when it succeeds) · repeat (one child, done again (`times`: how many; forever when not said)) · action (a leaf: something done) · condition (a leaf: something checked — true or false). Its lines are links: 'child' (`to` is a child of `from`; a node's children are tried in the order of its lines). Checked: It is a tree: each node has at most one parent, and none leads back to itself; It is one tree: no node is cut off from the rest.
- Syntax tree (formalism 'syntax-tree') — use it for: The structure of something written — a formula, a line of code, a query, a sentence — shown as parts within parts. Not for: Choosing what to do (a behaviour tree), or steps in an order (a process). Its members are nodes, of a kind: any kind you name. Its lines are links: 'child' (`to` is a part of `from`; parts are in the order of the lines, and `label` may say what part it is). Checked: It is a tree: each node has at most one parent, and none leads back to itself; It is one tree: no node is cut off from the rest.
